# Yönetici Özeti

A comparative evidence review of small modular reactor (SMR) projects in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Europe from 1 January 2023 to 30 July 2026 reveals a consistent pattern of optimistic design claims that are not substantiated by realized performance data. While SMRs are positioned as offering firm, dispatchable, carbon-free baseload power through standardized, modular designs, the evidence indicates significant unresolved technical, economic, and regulatory challenges. Key findings include: (1) SMR spent fuel is projected to be 50% more radiotoxic than conventional spent fuel after 10,000 years, challenging environmental and public acceptance narratives; (2) cost estimates—ranging from $4,000 to $7,000 per kW under nominal operating conditions (NOAK)—remain aspirational, with first-of-a-kind (FOAK) costs projected to be 30–50% higher and no real-world cost realizations or inflation-adjusted data; (3) the global nuclear supply chain has atrophied over three decades of minimal activity, creating a 'chicken-and-egg' dilemma between supply chain development and firm orders; (4) HALEU fuel dependency—dominated by Russia—introduces severe geopolitical and operational risks, with domestic enrichment capacity in North America and Europe underdeveloped; (5) NuScale’s project cancellation, with costs rising from $5.3 billion to $9.3 billion, illustrates uncontrolled cost escalation and financial fragility; (6) only three Western SMR designs are in advanced licensing or deployment stages, all based on light-water reactor (LWR) technology, which reduces regulatory risk but does not resolve underlying cost or supply chain issues; (7) no project-specific data on construction delays, cancellations, or financial overruns are available, and no independent, realized cost or performance benchmarks exist for operational SMRs in the region. These findings collectively suggest that SMRs are not yet commercially viable, with deployment constrained by unresolved waste, supply chain fragility, and a lack of demonstrated performance. The absence of verified data on cost, schedule, financing, or commercial maturity undermines confidence in industry claims and highlights a critical gap between design narratives and real-world outcomes.
