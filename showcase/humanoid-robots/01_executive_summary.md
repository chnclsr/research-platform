# Yönetici Özeti

The evidence is best interpreted by theme rather than as one universal conclusion. The body of evidence indicates that humanoid robots are currently in early-stage deployment within automotive manufacturing and logistics, primarily through pilot programs and limited operational integration rather than full-scale, verified production use. Figure Robotics is actively involved in pilot programs with automotive and technology manufacturers for inspection tasks, such as repetitive defect checks and component transfer, suggesting a focus on niche, high-repetition tasks rather than end-to-end manufacturing workflows. Agibot (Zhiyuan Robotics) has established a mass production hub in Shanghai and deployed over 1,500 units in less than a year, signaling a significant scaling effort in China, though the specific industrial applications—particularly in automotive manufacturing—are not detailed in the evidence. The claim that humanoid robots are recommended for industries with labor shortages or those operating in human-designed environments is supported by multiple sources, including Figure and S08, reinforcing their potential utility in logistics and manufacturing where labor is scarce or tasks are repetitive. However, no source provides verified data on actual cycle times, payload capacities, task success rates, or safety metrics from real-world production environments. While vision-language-action (VLA) models like RL$^2$-VLA are being developed to improve task success and action diversity in real-world settings, these remain theoretical or experimental frameworks, with no reported performance benchmarks in industrial settings. The evidence also confirms that logistics is leading in adoption, followed by healthcare, education, and retail, with no indication that humanoid robots have yet achieved economic viability or surpassed human workers in efficiency, safety, or reliability in production contexts. All claims about autonomous operation, commercial viability, or widespread deployment are either speculative or based on announcements rather than independently verified operational data. The absence of independent reporting, academic performance metrics, or official records from production facilities limits the ability to distinguish between pilot demonstrations and actual, verified integration. Thus, while humanoid robots are being developed and deployed in pilot programs, there is no evidence of widespread, verified integration into automotive manufacturing or general production lines during the specified period.
